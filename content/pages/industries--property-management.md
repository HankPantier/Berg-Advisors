Berg Advisors has spent more than 30 years building [outsourced accounting](/services/accounting) systems for landlords, property managers, and real estate holding companies who need that complexity organized, not oversimplified. Woodard named the firm a Top 50 Accounting Firm, and Intuit recognized it as a Firm of the Future, distinctions earned by handling exactly this kind of work.

Berg Advisors operates as a virtual firm, so a property manager in Newtown Square, Pennsylvania works with the same team, the same flat monthly fee, and the same proactive check-ins as one managing units across three states. ## The bookkeeping challenges unique to multi-unit portfolios

Property management accounting breaks standard small-business bookkeeping in specific ways. Rent collected for one owner's property can't mix with another's. Security deposits need to sit in separate trust accounts under state regulations. Management fees have to be calculated and posted correctly before owner distributions go out. Multiply that across several properties and a handful of LLCs, and one missed reconciliation can throw off owner statements for the entire month.

Most property managers we talk with are still tracking distributions in spreadsheets, reconciling bank feeds by hand, and finding out about a shortfall only after an owner asks why their check is light. Trust accounting adds another layer of risk: getting the separation between client funds and operating funds wrong isn't just messy bookkeeping, it's a compliance problem with a state regulator attached to it. These workflows need to be built around property management mechanics from the start, not retrofitted from a generic chart of accounts.
