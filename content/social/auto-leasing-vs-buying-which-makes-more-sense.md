## LinkedIn

A lease payment looks smaller for one reason: it only covers the vehicle's depreciation, not the full purchase price. Buy the same vehicle with a loan and you're financing the whole thing, which is why that payment runs higher even though you end up owning something.

For multi-channel sellers, the real numbers to weigh are mileage and timing. Most leases cap you at 10,000-15,000 miles a year, with penalties of 15-30 cents per mile over that. If you're running twice-weekly wholesale club trips or hauling product to a fulfillment center, you can blow past that cap by August. Buying also opens the door to a Section 179 deduction on vans, trucks, and SUVs over 6,000 lbs GVWR, while a lease only gets you a steady deduction on the business-use percentage.

Andrew Berg, CPA, breaks down the full framework, mileage patterns, Q4 cash flow, and balance sheet implications, so you can run your own numbers before the dealer runs theirs.
